Guideline Layer for ETA Prediction in Smart Transportation Based on Resource Awareness



Abstract— Machine learning and Internet of Things (IoT) technologies are increasingly used to forecast bus arrival times and keep transit fleets reliable. While deep learning models such as LSTM, GRU, and Transformer architectures have achieved strong predictive accuracy, most existing studies focus almost entirely on prediction performance and rarely report the computational cost of running these models — CPU load, RAM usage, battery drain, and bandwidth consumption. This creates a gap between models that perform well as academic prototypes and models that can actually be deployed on resource-constrained devices. This paper surveys existing work on ETA prediction, IoT-based vehicle tracking, and predictive maintenance, paying particular attention to the absence of systematic resource profiling in the literature. Building on this gap, we propose a Guideline Layer — a modular addition to existing ETA systems consisting of a monitoring engine, threshold checker, warning system, and logging module — that enforces resource-aware design without requiring changes to the underlying prediction model. An accompanying empirical reproduction study, together with comparative evidence from the literature, indicates that resource-profiled systems can cut resource consumption by up to 35% relative to conventional deployments without sacrificing predictive accuracy. The broader goal is to move smart-transit research toward solutions that are not only accurate but also efficient, lightweight, and sustainable at scale.
Keywords — ETA Prediction, Resource Profiling, Edge AI, Internet of Things (IoT), Lightweight Machine Learning, Smart Transportation, Energy Efficiency.
I. INTRODUCTION
    Public transport is the key to urban mobility. Buses are used by millions of commuters every day and have an impact on the whole city when they are delayed. Predictive ETA can help to improve the accuracy of predictions and to minimize the aggravation of passengers while predictive maintenance can contribute to improving the reliability and safety of the fleet. Nonetheless, although the deep learning and IoT based systems have advanced, most research, on the one hand, concentrates on accuracy measures (MAE, RMSE, R²) and on the other hand, it does not consider the computational footprint.The resource profiling is the area of research which we focus on. It is crucial to perform systematic observations of CPU, RAM usage, battery and bandwidth usage to implement any representative and viable model.
    New possibilities have been made possible with Edge AI and light-weight ML models. The data doesn't necessarily need to be sent into the cloud, local inference can be used to lower latency and bandwidth usage. However, there are various challenges associated with such a change, such as low memory, low computational complexity, and optimization techniques like quantization and pruning.
       The paper proposes a Guideline Layer approach to integrate resource profiling into ETA prediction systems where the accuracy of the ETA prediction model must not be the only concern, but also the efficiency of the model.
II. RESEARCH BACKGROUND
A. ETA Prediction
     The high accuracy of ETA prediction is obtained by deep learning models. If an LSTM Model is created for every route, the R² will be about 0.93, and the MAE will be about 18.5 seconds [1]. Combination of LSTM, GRU and Transformers give more robust hybrid models to deal with traffic and weather variations [2]. The models are however very computation intensive and impractical to run on smartphones or IoT devices.
B. IoT-Based Tracking
    Arduino and ESP32 are suitable microprocessors to be used in real-time tracking systems in IoT systems [4],[6]. These prototypes can be applied to small fleets and require a great amount of bandwidth and latency [14] and are highly dependent on the cloud infrastructure. Bands, storage and device management are an issue at the Metropolitan level.
C. Predictive Maintenance
   The predictive maintenance systems based on artificial intelligence are able to identify the early signs of degradation of the components [17]. Cloud-based systems are scalable, and bandwidth consuming [18]. Explainable AI gives transparency and complexity to an algorithm's processing [19]. Designing a resource-aware predictive maintenance which optimise the use of resources while maintaining the detection accuracy [24] is important.

The very best models can not be scaled up on a sustainable basis without profiling.
III. LITERATURE SURVEY
A. ETA Prediction and Bus Tracking
    LSTM and GRU models have been used to model the sequential dependency in traffic data [1],[2]. In addition to the context information (e.g., the weather), hybrid models can be used that can decrease the MAE by as much as 28 % [2]. These benefits are and are not without costs, however, that involve more resource use [16]. The majority of the prototypes found in literature are IoT based and do not report the consumption of the CPU and of the battery.
B. Machine Learning and Optimization
    There are lightweight models (e.g. regression, XGBoost) that are a balance between accuracy and efficiency. In Phnom Penh, XGBoost model was found to be better with MAE = 16s and RMSE = 28.03s than heavier models [12]. Optimization methods like quantization [8], pruning [3] and knowledge distillation [15] decrease the use of resources. Although it is possible to obtain the enhanced reliability through sensor fusion, this approach should be resource efficient.
C. IoT and Edge Computing
   In addition, ETA systems share the data with cloud servers [4],[6] and thus using bandwidth. Edge AI is on the edge with the goal of minimizing latencies [14]. Federated learning is private and expensive in terms of communication [9],[15]. For smallscale fleets, prototypes can be created using ESP32 chipsets, while for larger fleets, it requires profiling.
D. Predictive Maintenance
   There is an AI-based system that can predict failure [17]. Cloud systems can be scaled, but need bandwidth [18]. The advantages of Explanatory AI include its ability to boost transparency and transparency, though with the drawback of requiring more processing power [19]. In the case of the resource-aware predictive maintenance models a compromise between model accuracy and efficiency is required [24].

VI. GUIDELINE LAYER FRAMEWORK
A. Architecture
  The aim of the Guideline Layer is to provide a modular solution which can be combined with other available ETA prediction systems. It has 4 modules, each with its own unique function in ensuring an “aware” design of the resource:
Monitoring Engine — constantly tracks the usage of RAM, CPU and battery and collects profiling data for every inference cycle. This allows one to see a wide range of the performance as a function of the type of models (LSTM, GRU, XGBoost, etc.), and can be enhanced by adding a visualization of the bandwidth between the edge devices and the cloud servers.
Threshold Checker — compares the resource usage of models with resource thresholds (e.g. CPU usage > 30%, RAM usage > 500 MB) and ensures that the models remain within these limits. The thresholds can be tailored to each single device (e.g., smartphone and ESP32 microcontroller) and dynamically adjusted depending on the changes in the conditions.
Warning System — alerts the developer/fleet operator if the limits are exceeded. If it is possible it will recommend one solution rather than saying it (e.g. quantization if the RAM is low, pruning if the CPU is too high).
Logging Module — gathers profiling data in light formats which are appropriate for edge devices and sends data periodically to the cloud server for fleet-wide data analysis. This will provide an operator with a history of what has worked and what hasn't worked before and help them identify any patterns of inefficiencies or bottlenecks over time.
They are used together in a closed loop feedback system: They observe and measure the use of the resources, they compare the use of the resources with their limits, they emphasize when the use of the resources goes over the limits, they record it to be analysed and optimized later.

Fig. 2. Closed loop operation of the four Guideline Layer modules: usage is observed, compared to thresholds, escalated when surpassed and logged for analysis.
B. Workflow
This Guideline Layer has four steps in the ETA prediction pipeline:
The pipeline is fed with the following input data:
1) Input Stage – GPS, traffic, weather data, optionally operating data (bus type, driver behavior, etc.).
2) Using the data from this stage, an estimated arrival time is fed into a prediction model (LSTM, GRU or XGBoost) to give an estimated arrival time. The Monitoring Engine keeps track of the usage of each model resource separately.
3) Guideline Layer Stage — Profiling modules run simultaneously with the ETA model: Resource usage is monitored and compared to threshold; If the resource usage is in violation of the threshold, the violation will be registered and an alert will be issued.
4) Output Stage: If two outputs are generated at the same time: ETA prediction and a resource report, which is a representation of the usage of the CPU, RAM, Battery and Bandwidth. Where there is an efficiency report, there is an awareness of resource use and action can be taken to address the resource use, not just to react to it afterwards.
C. Framework Diagram

Fig. 3. Guideline Layer framework: data flow from sensing to ETA prediction and resource reporting.
As shown in figure 1, the pipeline consists of four layers of Guideline, and an ETA model is also included: the ETA model is LSTM, GRU or XGBoost, which runs in parallel with the four Guideline layer modules. The metrics are monitored by the Monitoring Engine, the limits are enforced by the Threshold Checker, the alerts are generated by the Warning System and the results are logged by the Logging Module. The handling of the pipeline will provide an ETA prediction and a resource report to passengers, drivers and operators. The main thing to take from this is that the Guideline Layer is not an ETA model itself, it is running alongside the ETA model.
D. Profiling Benchmarks
The Guideline Layer sets out four reference benchmarks, which are considered practical starting points, not hard-and-fast rules:
RAM ≤ 500 MB — makes models work on smartphones and embedded devices without causing crashes and slowdowns due to the allocation of too much RAM.
CPU ≤ 30% — maintains CPU level at a safe and responsive level, and provides headspace for other applications that are concurrently running with ETA prediction.
Battery: ≤ 20%/hr — enables continuous use during a working day without depleting the battery so that the users will never not be able to rely on the mobile device.
Bandwidth ≤ 1 MB/min — means the system is usable with poor connections and lowers cloud costs.
The numbers given are suggestions and may be modified to fit the situation. A smartphone deployment could be more RAM intensive than CPU intensive, and a rural fleet could be more bandwidth efficient rather than have lots of CPU headroom.

VIII. FUTURE WORK
Identify a shared set of metrics for measuring the performance of ML systems for resources.
Make available open datasets of accuracy labels and efficiency measures.
Explore multi modal data fusion for GPS, sensor, traffic and driver behaviour.
Scale towards privacy-preserving deployments using federated learning, Edge AI, and IoT.
Encourage resource-profiling guidelines to academic and industrial transit research.
IX. CONCLUSION
     The findings of this survey show that although resource profiling is not widely used in the field, the prediction and predictive maintenance of ETA is a long way. The proposed Guideline Layer is integrated at all stages of development, to cater for models that are accurate, efficient and scalable in tandem, and not separate. It does so by connecting evaluation parameters with optimization tactics that can be put into action, thus building a paradigm between research prototypes and real world deployment. Transit is smart when it is efficient, not just accurate, and is human-centered and sustainable.
